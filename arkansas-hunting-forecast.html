Arkansas season timing estimates

At about 34.9 degrees north, Arkansas falls where the model puts the deer rut about 19 days later than the northern reference peak of Nov 12. These markers come from a latitude-based model and are not wildlife agency data. Elevation, local herds and weather shift real timing, and hunting seasons, bag limits and legal hours are set by the Arkansas wildlife agency, so check their current regulations before you go.

How the forecast works

Each hour gets a score from 0 to 100. Light and time of day carry the most weight, followed by weather factors that differ by species: barometric pressure trend, temperature and cold fronts, wind, rain and cloud cover, moon phase, and a season calendar estimated from your latitude.

Weather comes from the free Open-Meteo service for your exact location, so the numbers update as the forecast changes. The scores are rules of thumb drawn from hunting research and field reports. They are not a validated model, and nothing replaces local knowledge. Use the hunt log to compare the score with what you actually see.

Keep your own hunt log

Pick a day and the forecast numbers for it load automatically. Record when you hunted, whether you saw anything and when, and your notes. After a few hunts the log shows how your results line up with the scores. Your log stays on your device.
